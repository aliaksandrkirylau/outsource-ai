PM Copilot Field Guide

Twenty-one skills for running the PMO delivery standard. What each one gives you, when to reach for it, and what to type.

Read this first

You don't launch skills. You write to Claude in normal words, describe your situation, and it picks the right one. Nothing on this page is a command you have to memorise - the phrases in the last column are examples, not syntax.

How it plays out

Four ordinary weeks. What you type, what the copilot does, and every place it stops and waits for you.

Sales hands you a new project

Runs a01-presale-handover - six stages, and it stops after each one.

“I've been handed Beta, here's the pre-sale chat and the folder”

  1. It reads the file list first, nothing deeper:

    It asks“Found 14 sources. Is this the right handover material?”

  2. Then it reads properly and leads with the client's problem, not the feature list. If the material never says why the client came to us, it marks that UNCLEAR and makes it your first question for Sales.
  3. Stage 3 gives you numbers, calculated by a script, not eyeballed:
    ≥ 70%Handover Maturity Index
    ≤ 30%Critical Uncertainty Ratio
    Plus a Sheet of the 18 core checklist items, each linked to the exact slide or cell it came from. It doesn't send the Sheet. You send it to Sales to confirm line by line.
  4. Stage 4 is the step's one hard gate:

    Hard gate“I'll put these 9 documents in the project brain and hold back the budget Sheet and the SOW. OK?”

    The notebook gets created only after a clear yes.

Afterwards it knows A01 is done, so “what's next on Beta?” picks up from there rather than starting over.

Something worrying comes up on a call

No initiation step here. risk-register, update mode.

“client said their DevOps is off for a month in November”

  1. It answers what you actually asked, then names the skill you just wandered into:

    It offers“That's a risk to the release. Want it in the Risk Register as K-7?”

  2. One row: severity, owner, response, and whether it's critical. A risk with no owner isn't logged yet, and it will tell you so.
  3. It shows the row and what it does to the coverage number, then stops:

    It asks“Adding K-7. That makes 3 of 4 critical risks owned. Filing it to Confluence - OK?”

    It files wherever your register already lives, Jira or Confluence.

You never re-run initiation to log one risk. The new row is offered to the project brain in the same message.

You inherit a project that's been running for months

Runs map-existing-project, not the router.

“I took over Gamma, it's been running since March”

  1. Why not the router: scanning a live project's folder finds almost nothing, and the router would read that as a project that never started and send it back to pre-sale handover.
  2. A read-only sweep of Drive, Chat, mail, Jira, Confluence, Calendar and NotebookLM. You see the inventory before any conclusions, then a source register.
  3. Then what's on fire, worst first: overdue promises, a CR agreed in chat and never processed, a card nobody has updated. Each one goes into your task list.
  4. Last, a score against the mandatory PMO artifacts, and it asks rather than assumes:

    It asks“12 confirmed. For the 6 I couldn't find, do they exist somewhere I didn't look?”

Nothing reaches a shared system before Stage 6 - only your own task list. Not finding something is never reported as missing until you've answered that question.

It's time for the weekly status

Runs prorep-card, one card or your whole portfolio.

“do my weekly statuses”

  1. It asks which projects and how you want to feed it. Express: you give it the week in a few lines. Full sweep: it gathers the evidence from chats, Jira and mail itself.
  2. It drafts the eight-field status and puts the eight indicators next to what's on the card now. Only the ones that should change get flagged. Carrying a value forward is normal.

    Hard gate“Two of the eight would change: Delivery Health to Yellow, Project Stage to UAT, and why. Post it?”

  3. After it posts, it gives you the same text ready to paste into the project's strategy chat, in the language that chat runs in.

The card is visible to leadership, so nothing goes on it without a clear yes. The chat post is always yours to send.

copilot-setup
What you getYour connectors probed and repaired, the copilot's rules and its copy of the PMO standard installed, your profile drafted, a task list and a feedback log started.
WhenFirst time you use the copilot - and again whenever something that worked last week has stopped answering.
  • “set up my copilot”
  • “Claude can't see my Drive any more”
map-existing-project
What you getA sweep of Drive, Chat, Gmail, Jira, Confluence and Calendar into a source register, a takeover brief, what needs attention now, and a score against the mandatory PMO artifacts.
WhenA project has been running for months and Claude has never seen a file from it. Also when you've just inherited one.
  • “get this project into Claude”
  • “I took over Philmore, catch me up”
project-router
What you getWhere the project stands against the standard, which outputs already exist, how ready it is for the gate, and which skill to run next.
WhenA project that's genuinely new, or one already mapped, and you want to know what's next rather than guess.
  • “what should I do next on Alchemy?”
  • “what's left before we can start?”
standards-sync
What you getA refreshed copy of the standard from live Confluence, separating what actually changed from what the copilot had wrong.
WhenA Confluence page reads differently from what the copilot just told you. If you think it's out of date, you're probably right.
  • “sync the standards”
  • “Artem updated the standard”

The initiation arc - before the gate

a01-presale-handoverA01
What you getA handover brief, the commitments and assumptions Sales made, a scored checklist as a Sheet you can send them to fill the gaps, a source register and a first risk log.
WhenSales has handed a sold project over and nobody has worked out what was actually promised.
  • “I got a handover”
  • “what did pre-sale hand over?”
a02-a04-stakeholders-and-descriptionA02 · A04
What you getA stakeholder and communication register with a decision-ownership table, and the project description.
WhenNobody can say who approves what, or a newcomer asks what this project actually is and there's no written answer.
  • “who signs off on what here?”
  • “write the project description”
a03-contract-commitmentsA03
What you getA commitments register - delivery obligations, client obligations, acceptance rules, reporting duties - checked against the scope, roadmap and economy.
WhenThe signed contract or SOW lands and nobody has read it against what the team plans to build.
  • “what did we commit to?”
  • “does the contract match our scope?”
a05-a06-scope-and-risksA05 · A06
What you getThe assumptions behind the estimate interrogated, critical scope with acceptance criteria, a required-roles list, CR candidates, and the project's first risk register.
WhenThe sold estimate looks thin and you have to say so before committing to it.
  • “are we underestimating this?”
  • “sold vs what we can actually deliver”
a07-a08-roadmap-and-economyA07 · A08
What you getA roadmap and milestones with owners and dates, the role-by-month hours plan, and those hours written into the economy file for you and the RM to work on.
WhenYou owe a client or leadership dates and have none, or the economy file is empty so staffing can't be requested.
  • “build the roadmap”
  • “how long will this take?”
a09-baseline-gateA09 · the gate
What you getThe baseline package, a one-page leadership summary carrying your readiness call and your flags, the value classification, and the decision recorded.
WhenScope, plan and economy are on the table and someone has to say go or no-go. Setup and staffing don't begin before this.
  • “run the baseline gate”
  • “is this project approved to start?”

The initiation arc - after the gate

a10-staffingA10
What you getThe role requirements you send the Delivery Manager, a checklist for accepting candidates, and the confirmed team moved into your Resource Plan and P&L.
WhenYou're about to ask for people, or the DM has come back and the team needs recording.
  • “what roles do I need to staff?”
  • “the DM sent role requirements”
a11-project-setupA11
What you getDrive folders and sensitive storage, the project notebook, a chat plan, the board checked, the PROREP card's descriptive fields filled, and Communication Plan and Working Rules drafts.
WhenThe project has nowhere to put anything, or what it has is a mess. Worth doing early - earlier steps assume these exist.
  • “the project has no folders”
  • “is my project set up properly?”
a12-a14-kickoffA12 – A14
What you getInternal team alignment, the PMO kick-off deck filled slide by slide, the client process topics, an access list, the invite, and the 24-hour follow-up.
WhenKick-off is on the calendar and there's no deck, agenda or follow-up yet.
  • “prepare the kick-off”
  • “write the kick-off recap”

All project long

prorep-cardweekly · every project
What you getThe eight-field status and the eight PMO indicators, posted to the leadership card in one go, plus the same text handed back for you to post in the project chat.
WhenIt's the weekly reporting slot. Or a card hasn't been touched in weeks while the project moved on.
  • “do my weekly statuses”
  • “which of my cards are stale?”
risk-register
What you getOne risk or blocker in or out, holding the register's IDs and conventions - plus a review of whether your criticals still have owners.
WhenSomething worrying comes up mid-delivery. Or the register hasn't moved in months and you want to know what's rotted.
  • “add a risk: the client's API is undocumented”
  • “review my risk register”
project-brain
What you getThings worth remembering pushed into the project's NotebookLM notebook, answers pulled back out of it, and a check on whether it's gone stale.
WhenSomething worth keeping was just produced or found. Or nobody can recall what was decided and when.
  • “did we ever decide about the mobile scope?”
  • “is the brain up to date?”
correspondence-log
What you getThe project's client email collected into one Doc the team can read and the notebook can answer from, back over the history and forward from now on. It makes mail findable; it is not evidence of anything.
WhenYou are digging through your own inbox for what a client agreed. Or the project's mail lives nowhere but one mailbox and somebody else now needs that trail.
  • “collect the correspondence for this project”
  • “where is the email where they agreed to that?”

Whenever you ask or write

pmo-guide
What you getThe rule, where it is written and whether it is current - read from the live Confluence page with the page ID cited. Any block of the standard, A to G. It explains and points you at the skill that does the work.
WhenYou need to know how we do something here, and the answer has to be the current one rather than what someone remembers.
  • “who approves a change request?”
  • “what does the standard say for a running project?”
pm-voice
What you getText you can paste: the length the channel expects, without the tells that make a message read as AI, in English or Russian. Your own voice skill or saved samples win over its defaults.
WhenAnything that goes out with your name on it. Other skills hand their drafts to it, so mostly you won't call it at all.
  • “reply to the client, keep it short”
  • “this reads like AI, fix it”
daily-digest
What you getOne digest a day of your Chat spaces, mail and meeting notes - what needs you, what was decided, new ideas and use cases, risks - delivered to a private place you choose. You pick what it looks for and when it runs; it asks for the time at the end of setup.
WhenYou skim a hundred spaces and still miss the one decision that mattered. Or you re-scroll every chat at the end of the day. It runs while Claude is open.
  • “set up a daily digest of my chats”
  • “add risks on the Alchemy project to my digest”
wrap-up
What you getBefore a chat closes: new tasks on your list, what stays true in the project’s context file, and a short resume note in the project folder, so the next chat starts where this one stopped. A wrap-up routine of your own wins over it.
WhenYou are done for the day, or moving to a new chat. It writes only your own local files - never Jira, PROREP or a message.
  • “wrap up”
  • “that’s it for today, let’s close”

What it will not do

Worth knowing up front, so none of it reads as the copilot failing.

  • It never sends. Not to a client, not to Sales, not to a project chat. It drafts and names the destination; you send. The one exception is your own daily digest, posted only to the private place you chose for it.
  • It shows you every write first. Nothing reaches Jira, a shared Sheet, a P&L or the project notebook without the exact content on screen and a yes from you.
  • It doesn't approve anything. It prepares the decision and records what was decided. The call is yours.
  • Money stays out of the project notebook, the common folder and anything client-facing. Hours are fine everywhere; rates, margins and P&L figures are not.
  • Contract text lives in a separate confidential notebook, never the project brain.
  • It is not an authority on how we do things. When it says “I'm not sure what we usually do here, this is my draft” - believe it, and check with the people who hold the convention.

Getting more out of it

Six things that make the difference in practice.

  • Describe the situation, not a command. “The client just told me something that worries me” works better than any skill name.
  • Name the project. It knows your portfolio and the aliases each project goes by, so one name is enough.
  • It works in stages and stops. Every checkpoint shows what it found before it moves on. “Go on” is all it needs.
  • Ask what it can do - it answers against your own portfolio. “What can you help me with?” gets what's overdue on your projects, not a list of features. Needs the copilot's rules installed, which is step 8 of setup.
  • Say “log this” when it gets something wrong. It keeps a feedback log in your workspace - send the file back and the next version is better.
  • Two files do the remembering: project-context.md per project, and one task list across your whole portfolio. It writes to both as it goes and tells you when it did.
