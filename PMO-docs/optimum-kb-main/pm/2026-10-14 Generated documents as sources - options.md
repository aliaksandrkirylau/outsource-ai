## Four relationships an artifact can have with the KB

1. **View.** It is built from notes and filed where the standard says. The KB is the master; the filed copy is
   never edited, and a change to it comes back as a source through ingest. The risk register is the model. A
   view is never ingested itself.
2. **Record.** It is the trace of an act: something was agreed, approved, sent or accepted. The version that
   was agreed or sent is stored once, frozen, as a source. It is evidence of the act, and never of what it
   restates.
3. **Knowledge with no type.** The artifact carries facts the KB needs but has no type for. The stakeholder
   register (who on the client side decides what) and the RACI (who is accountable for what) are the cases.
4. **Outside.** State, money, people data and operations. The KB links them and never copies them.

**A view becomes a record the moment it is sent or accepted.** This is the point the first version missed.
Under a fixed price with written acceptance of each deliverable (COM-002), the client accepts one particular
version. A view regenerated next week is not that version. So options 4 and 2 below are not alternatives:
generate the artifact from the notes, then freeze whatever went out.

A generated document holds two kinds of content:

- **Restated content** is what the KB already has. It is never evidence a second time, wherever it turns up.
- **New content** is the choices made while writing it: who is A on a RACI row, the discovery schedule. Until
  someone agrees, these are our proposal (`origin: envisioned`). Once someone agrees, the evidence is the
  agreement, not our text.

Sending is not agreement (rule 10). A sent plan proves what we proposed and when. The client's written OK is a
separate source, if it comes.

## Options

### 1. Leave it in pm/ and let the event carry the knowledge

This is today's practice. The document stays in `pm/`. When it is agreed, the event is ingested, the new
choices go into typed notes, and those notes link the `pm/` file at the commit that was agreed.

- For: no schema change. Rule 1 is read literally.
- Against:
  - The 33 rows of the RACI and an accepted Vision and Scope would exist in full only in a folder that agents
    do not autoload and may not cite.
  - A link "at commit 887de0c" does not open in Obsidian.
  - Nobody can tell later which version was agreed, and that is the one question a fixed-price acceptance
    dispute asks.

### 2. Freeze the agreed or sent version as a source, marked as ours

This handles every record in the map.

- **Sent to the client:** store the text as it went, taken from the e-mail rather than from `pm/exports/`.
  - In the body, it goes into the thread's next rendering in `sources/client/`, as the 14 Oct e-mail already
    does.
  - As an attachment, it gets its own source next to the thread.
- **Agreed internally:** store it in `sources/internal/<date> <name>/`.
- **The overview gets two fields:**
  - `generated_from`: the file or export, the commit, and the notes it was built from.
  - `issued`: who agreed, sent or accepted it, and the event that shows it.
- **The trust note gains one line:** this is ours. It is evidence of the act and its date, never of what it
  restates.

Trade-offs:

- For: it is ingest step 3 applied to our own documents. The version is frozen and citable, and circularity is
  fenced by a field.
- Against: rule 1 needs one more sentence, and the two fields are a schema change. The guard is only as good as
  the agent filling the field.

### 3. A folder of its own: sources/issued/

Everything we issue goes to one folder with one trust note.

- For: an agent knows from the path alone that the text is ours.
- Against:
  - It splits threads: our message would sit in one folder and the client's reply in another.
  - The presale documents we sent sit elsewhere.
  - It is the largest change of the four.

### 4. Generated views: the content lives in notes, and the artifact is regenerated

This handles every view in the map, and it is the risk-register rule applied to everything. The filed copy is
never edited, and comments come back through ingest.

- For: it is Anton's D9, and it leaves one master.
- Against: there is one generator per artifact to build, and `procedures/export.md` needs a variant for filed
  views that keep the IDs the PMO uses (K-n). On its own it loses what was sent; combined with option 2 it does
  not.

## How Zeppla did it

Zeppla is our earlier KB in `8allocate_Zeppla`. I only read it.

- **Three tiers of trust by folder.** `00_drafts` is "Private, local, non-authoritative drafts". In
  `04_artifactory`, "All artifacts in the folder are not trusted". `02_wiki` holds the verified knowledge.
- **The only promotion rule is for ADRs.** "After the decision is made, move only the development-relevant
  Executive Summary into the Wiki" (`CLAUDE.md`). We already copied this as the Decision and `tl/` rule.
- **What was presented was kept, but unmarked.** Copies sit in meeting folders ("Presented ADRs", the customer
  decision briefs) with no status, no sent date and no link back, while the artifactory version kept changing.
- **No provenance field anywhere.** The meeting-notes skill lists ADRs brought to a session among the
  meeting's sources, as "useful but unverified context". Nothing tracks that our own text has come back.
- **Citing working material breaks.**
  - 81 wiki notes cite JSON files in `00_drafts`, which is gitignored, and those files no longer exist.
  - Accepted decisions link evaluations in a folder whose "Trusted" list says "No trusted artifacts yet".

For the BA's artifacts, which matter most for us next, I traced one user story, US-5.2, end to end:

- **Four copies, no named master.**
  - The story was drafted in `Claude outputs/`.
  - It was reworked in the BA's space the same day.
  - It was rewritten by a person in Outline, the documentation site shared with the client.
  - It was exported one way back into the KB (`07_outline/`).
  - The wiki Feature holds no story text and no link to it.
- **The client-shared copy became the master by practice, not by rule.** The client said "The shared
  documentation site is the source of truth for documented project decisions and conflict resolution" (team
  sync, 1 Sep). After that:
  - The BA's workshop list says "Source of truth: Outline - Requirements Space".
  - The Sprint 5 specs cite the Outline export as their "Source of truth".
  - No KB rule says any of this.
- **The AI pass closed questions that the human pass reopened.** The reworked draft settled two open questions
  internally ("screenshot wins over the text table") and recorded "No open items remain". The Outline version
  reopened one of them, and its title still says "(URL generation is open)". This is an assumption presented as
  a fact, made by an agent, which is exactly what our rule 9 forbids.
- **Approval was never recorded.** All 46 stories in the export have an empty "Approval (Yes/No, name and
  date)" cell, and every wiki Feature and Requirement is still a candidate.
- **The export was edited locally.** A later commit changed the Outline export in the KB, so the KB's copy no
  longer matches what the client sees.
- **Client answers never came back as typed knowledge.** There is one Answer entity, against workshop lists and
  follow-ups kept in the BA's space.

Lesson: cite only what is frozen, and mark what is ours. Keep what went out apart from what we are still working
on. Above all, decide where the BA's master copy lives before the BA starts and the client is given a shared
space; otherwise the shared space decides it for us.

## Circularity, whichever option

1. **Mark it at birth.**
   - `pm/` files open with "written from the KB at commit ...", and exports carry `built_from`.
   - A view filed in Confluence says at the top that it is generated and must not be edited there.
   - Nothing is marked in what the client receives. For that text, `pm/exports/` is the record.
2. **Check it at ingest.**
   - Before extracting from a message, check whether the text is ours, by searching a distinctive phrase in
     `pm/` and `pm/exports/`.
   - If it is ours, record "no material - KB echo", and extract only the reactions to it: an agreement, an
     objection, a correction, a question, or an edit someone made along the way.
   - Views are never ingested. A comment on a view is.
3. **An echo is never a second source.** A fact confirmed only by our own text stays our view. A client
   confirmation needs the client's own words.
4. **A generator never closes a Question.** What is open in the notes stays open in the view, and an
   `[ASSUMPTION: ...]` stays visible (Zeppla's US-5.2 shows why).

## Who promotes

- **A view:** its owner files it. The PMs file the registers, the description and the roadmap; the BA files
  Vision and Scope and the backlog; the TL files the architecture. Nothing in a view is promoted by filing it.
- **A record:** the person running the ingest approves the stored version at ingest step 7.
- **The choices in it:** rule 2 is unchanged. Deciders verify Decisions. A PM verifies the Commitments touched
  by a client acceptance, and only once the client's written OK is stored (rule 10).
- **The rule itself:** the curator, logged.

## Recommendation

Use the four relationships as the rule, with options 4 and 2 as stages of one flow:

1. Draft the artifact in `pm/` or `ba/`.
2. Generate it from the notes (option 4).
3. File it where the standard says, and never edit the filed copy.
4. Freeze what was agreed, sent or accepted as a source (option 2).
5. Ingest the reactions only.

Option 1 stays as the fallback for anything not filed. Option 3 is not needed.
